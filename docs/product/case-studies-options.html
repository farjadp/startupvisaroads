A new section that explains Federal Court judicial reviews of IRCC refusals (Type A) and, with consent, anonymised VisaRoads client cases (Type B). Each option below shows the same content three ways: the index at /en/case-studies, the single case page for 2025 FC 12, and how it maps to the app. Pick one direction; high fidelity comes after.

Refused? Read how the Court looked at cases like yours.

The Federal Court does not hear an appeal of a visa refusal and does not give anyone a visa. It only does a judicial review: it checks whether the officer's decision was reasonable and made fairly. If it was not, the file goes back to a different officer for a new decision.

Each case links the official decision. Applicants are never named.

Start-up visa work permit refused for "missing" language results that were on file

In one line: the officer refused because the language results were not provided, but they were in the file, so the Court sent the case back.

¶ 25[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]The decision rests on a finding that the record contradicts.

01Facts

The applicant held a commitment from a designated organisation and applied for a work permit under R205(a) while the start-up visa application was in process.

¶ 3[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]The Applicant applied for a work permit in support of an application under the Start-up Business Class.
The officer refused, writing that the language test results had not been submitted.
¶ 6[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]The officer noted that no language test results were provided.

02The issue

Was the refusal reasonable when its main reason did not match what was in the record?

¶ 12[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]The determinative issue is whether the decision is reasonable.

03What the Court held

The judicial review was allowed. The results were in the record the officer had, and the reasons did not account for them.

¶ 22[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]The language results form part of the certified tribunal record.
The matter goes back to a different officer.
¶ 30[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]The matter is remitted for redetermination by a different officer.

04Why the officer erred

A decision is unreasonable when it ignores or misstates evidence that goes to the heart of the reason given. Here the reason given was the missing document, and the document was there.

¶ 25[illustrative placeholder — not a quote]A decision that fails to account for evidence contradicting its central finding cannot stand.

05Lessons for your file

  • Name every upload so an officer finds it in seconds, and say in the cover letter where each document is.
  • Keep the upload confirmation for each document. It is your proof that something was on file.
  • If a refusal says something is missing that you sent, read the deadlines for leave in IRPA s. 72(2)(b) the same day.

Editorial long-read

Argument: a refusal is a story with a turning point, and people read stories to the end.
